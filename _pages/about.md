  <div class="hero-copy">
    <h1 class="hero-title" id="hero-heading">Zhen Wei</h1>
    <p class="hero-subtitle">Neuroscience and Psychology</p>
    <div class="hero-description">
      <p>I received an M.S. in Neuroscience from The University of Texas at Austin, where I studied computational models of primate visual perception under the supervision of <a href="https://liberalarts.utexas.edu/psychology/faculty/wsg8">Wilson Geisler</a> and <a href="https://liberalarts.utexas.edu/psychology/faculty/es58">Eyal Seidemann</a>. My research focused on figure–ground perception and edge detection, using visual psychophysics and optical imaging to investigate the neural mechanisms of macaque V1.</p>
      <p>Previously, I completed an M.A. in Psychology at New York University as a member of the <a href="https://wp.nyu.edu/landylab/">Landy Lab</a>, advised by <a href="https://as.nyu.edu/faculty/michael-s-landy.html">Michael Landy</a> and co-advised by <a href="https://as.nyu.edu/faculty/weiji-ma.html">Wei Ji Ma</a>. There, I investigated Bayesian causal inference of audiovisual localization. I received my B.S. in Psychology from Jilin University, where my undergraduate thesis examined perceptual grouping in visual crowding.</p>
